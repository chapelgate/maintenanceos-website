## One-sentence explanation

**MaintenanceOS by Chapelgate helps UK landlords, letting agents, property managers, and maintenance teams capture maintenance requests, structure the work, escalate critical cases, and keep approvals with authorised people.**

This is the canonical plain-English explanation. The product remains pre-launch. The AI receptionist capability is under testing and must not be presented as a public always-on phone service.

## Primary promise

**Turn maintenance requests into clear, controlled work.**

The promise is about the operational outcome, not the model or architecture.

## Messaging hierarchy

Every major buyer-facing first screen should answer, in this order:

1. **Outcome:** what problem is being made easier or more controlled.
2. **Audience:** UK landlords, letting agents, property managers, maintenance teams, or the page-specific role.
3. **Plain-English mechanism:** capture, structure, escalate, review, approve.
4. **Control proof:** server rules and authorised humans retain safety/approval authority.
5. **Status honesty:** pre-launch and any UNDER TEST limitation remains visible.

Do not lead a buyer page with implementation labels such as VERIFIED, UNDER TEST, deterministic, sandbox, or internal funnel mechanics when a plain outcome can lead instead. Those labels remain available immediately after the outcome where they prove honesty.

## Technical architecture boundary

Technical controls are proof, not the primary sales message. The homepage keeps a dedicated **How MaintenanceOS keeps control behind the scenes** section for AI intake, server rules, and human clearance. Customer benefits are presented separately above that section.

## Acceptance rule

Step 3 passes only when source tests, production build, and cache-bypassed live checks all prove the canonical headline/explanation and major-page clarity rules.
